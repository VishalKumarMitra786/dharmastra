# Dharmastra: the story frame

## The frame
Label: Invented, Legend. The chakra of time is our own storytelling device. It borrows the Indian idea of time as a turning wheel of ages (see the Tradition entry below), but the traveller and the walk are original.

The player is a traveller, female or male, named by the player. In text and captions the name appears as {name}. The traveller walks the chakra of time on foot. Each era, at the moment it needs a message carried, has no one free to carry it, and the traveller steps in as its courier. The traveller never rides a chariot or cart, and no epic figure or ruler appears on screen (docs/decisions.md, item 1). The traveller is not a hero of the war or the court. The errand is small, human and local, and the line the player hears each time is "No glory in this. Just a road that stayed open."

Tone: quiet, respectful, practical. No community is a villain. Deities and sacred traditions are only referred to, briefly and with respect.

## Eras (as they exist now)
Source of truth: missions/dvapara-escape.json and missions/mauryan-spy.json. Full notes are in screenplays/.

### Dvapara Yuga: "Escape from Hastinapur"
The traveller arrives at night, on foot, in Hastinapur, on the tense eve of the great war. A courier named Suvrata holds a sealed plea from farm villages asking for grain relief. The traveller carries it through the Lamp Bazaar, across the canal bridge before the watch bells close it, and to the east gate before dawn. Captain Dandaka reads the plea and opens the gate himself, because his own kin farm those fields.
- Hastinapur Period II, Painted Grey Ware occupation (B. B. Lal), c. 1100-800 BCE. History.
- Hastinapur 2021-22 excavation reports an earlier Painted Grey Ware layer, c. 1500-1000 BCE (as reported). History.
- Peace embassy to Hastinapura before the Kurukshetra war. Tradition, Legend.
- Traditional start of Kali Yuga, ending the Dvapara Yuga. Tradition, Legend.
- The traveller carries the grain plea out of the city. Invented, Legend.

### Mauryan empire: "The Night Report of Pataliputra"
On a monsoon night in timber-walled Pataliputra, the traveller takes a sealed report from the ferry clerk Vasuka at the Ganga wharf. The route runs past the timber yard and the palisade lane, dodging a doubled night watch, to Overseer Kshema at the moat sluice house. The feared secret is only a jammed sluice and a swelling river, and the warning lets the crews shore up the wall before morning.
- Megasthenes' account of Pataliputra's wooden wall and wide ditch (his book is lost and survives through later writers), c. late 4th to early 3rd century BCE (as reported, not verified). History.
- Waddell's excavations find remains of a timber palisade near Patna, 1892-1899 CE. History.
- Ashokan edict records wells dug and trees planted along roads, c. 257 BCE (as reported). History.
- Arthashastra, traditionally credited to Kautilya, with a chapter on spies. Authorship and date contested. Tradition, Contested.
- The traveller carries a ferry clerk's flood warning across the city. Invented, Legend.

### The chakra itself
- Time as a repeating wheel of yugas, ending in the Kali Yuga. Tradition, Legend.
- The traveller who walks it as a courier. Invented, Legend.

## Needs scholarly review
- Hastinapur 2021-22 excavation date (c. 1500-1000 BCE): taken from a Wikipedia summary. The excavation report (Garnayak, Puratattva 2022) was not read.
- Megasthenes' date and his description of the Pataliputra wall: known only through later writers, and the date of his embassy was not checked against a source.
- Ashokan edict date (c. 257 BCE) and wording: taken from a Wikipedia summary, no primary translation read.
- Arthashastra dating and authorship: several views exist and only summaries were read. The text forbids wood in fortifications, which is in tension with the timber palisade.
- Link between Waddell's palisade remains and the Mauryan wall: rests on scholars such as Kosmin (as reported). Waddell's own report was not read.
- Period labels for the Kali Yuga start and the peace embassy: Tradition, versions of the epic differ. No date given for the war.
- Whether the yuga cycle may be called a "wheel" or "chakra" of time in the sense used here: the wording is our own, and a reviewer familiar with the traditions should confirm it is respectful.
