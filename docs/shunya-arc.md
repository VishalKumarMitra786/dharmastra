# Shunya arc: from nothing to a way to protect others

Status: design draft for CEO review. Nothing here is in missions/*.json yet.
Label key: History, Tradition or Invented, each with a year or "Legend". Dialogue uses {name} and is at most 140 characters.

## The idea of "shunya" (Invented, Legend)
In India, shunya is the word for zero: the empty place that lets every other number have a value (History, the rules for zero are set out by Brahmagupta in 628 CE; the dot used as a placeholder in older texts is Tradition, date contested). In Dharmastra it is only a starting point for the traveller. {name} begins with no weapon and no skill, not as a lack but as an open place where learning can be put. The arc borrows the humility of that idea and nothing more: it makes no claim about the religious and philosophical meanings of shunya or shunyata, which are left alone out of respect. Every skill is earned from an ordinary teacher, is used only to defend oneself and to protect others, and never to hurt. The label for the whole frame is Invented, Legend. It sits inside the chakra of time in docs/story.md and obeys docs/decisions.md, item 1: no real deity or epic figure on screen, no one a target.

## Rules for all five eras
- Defence only. A skill stops, blocks, steers or slows trouble. Violence is restrained (CEO update, 2026-11-22): at most a brief impact mark or a small blood spatter, never graphic, never lingering, no dismemberment, and no one is killed on screen. Most "hits" stay a stumble, a dropped lantern or a lost step. Sacred traditions, deities and epic figures are never shown being harmed or mocked.
- Teachers are invented mortal people, labelled Invented. Real practices are named only as inspiration and labelled Tradition or History.
- The weapon is always taught with restraint first: when to put it down matters as much as how to hold it.
- Nothing is a villain. Hurdles are crowds, water, animals, dark, hurry and fear.
- Each era has one non-checkpoint objective, so the skill is used for people and not only to reach a gate.
- The skill order is also the era order: hands, evasion, staff, sword, bow. The traveller keeps all earlier skills.

## Era 1. Dvapara Yuga, "Escape from Hastinapur": bare hands (shunya: no skill)
- Setting labels: Hastinapur Period II, c. 1100-800 BCE (History); the great war's eve (Tradition, Legend); the grain-plea courier (Invented, Legend). See docs/story.md.
- Earns: steady stance, a firm grip-break, and how to fall and get up. Learned from Suvrata (Invented, Legend), the courier whose plea {name} carries. She is not a fighter; she teaches what a night courier needs when crowded. Inspiration: the idea that training starts with stance before any weapon, as in Kerala's kalari training order (Tradition, documented since about the 12th century CE, origins disputed; used only as inspiration, no technique copied).
- Hurdle: the Lamp Bazaar crush. A crowd surges and a stranger grabs the traveller's sleeve in panic. The player learns to turn the wrist, free the sleeve and step back without striking. A held lamp must not go out.
- Objective (protect): keep a child's paper lantern lit and the child beside {name} through the bazaar. The lantern is lost if the player stumbles. This objective is not a checkpoint.
- Dialogue: Suvrata: "{name}, you hold nothing yet. Good. Then hold your footing first, and the rest will find a place." (97 characters)

## Era 2. Mauryan empire, "The Night Report of Pataliputra": evasion
- Setting labels: timber palisade and ditch at Pataliputra, c. late 4th to early 3rd century BCE (History, as reported); the ferry wharf and the flood-warning courier (Invented, Legend). See docs/story.md.
- Earns: evasion: ducking under a rope, slipping between a watch's turns, going still in shadow, reading a guard's lantern. Learned from Revati (Invented, Legend), a ferry-rope hand who knows every gap in the wharf at night. Inspiration: the Arthashastra's chapters on watchmen and spies are Tradition, Contested (authorship and date disputed); only the idea that a careful person sees without being seen is borrowed.
- Hurdle: the doubled night watch in the palisade lane. The player must wait for a lantern to swing away, cross open ground and stop short of the light. Being seen costs a lost step and a longer way round, not a fight.
- Objective (rescue): on the way to the sluice house, lead a stranded night-market porter out of a flooded lane to higher ground, using only quiet movement. The porter follows the traveller's shadow.
- Dialogue: Revati: "{name}, the watch looks where it is used to looking. Be where it is not, and you owe no one a fight." (100 characters)

## Era 3 (proposed). Tamilakam, Sangam age, hill and harbour road: the staff
- Setting labels: Sangam period, c. 300 BCE to 300 CE (History, as reported, dates of the poems debated). Bamboo staffs sold with swords and armour to foreign traders, as reported for the silambam tradition (Tradition; no exact date, flagged). Muziris-style pepper harbour (History, c. 1st century CE, as reported, not checked here). The caravan and the harbour road are Invented, Legend.
- Earns: the staff: a long reach that keeps distance, turns a charge aside, steadies a rope bridge, and sounds an alarm when tapped. Learned from Poonkodi (Invented, Legend), a hill bamboo cutter who shapes each staff to its owner's height. Inspiration: silambam, named after the silambu bamboo of the hills (Tradition, said to be practised since at least the 4th century BCE, as reported). No move sequence is copied.
- Hurdle: a swollen hill stream with a rope bridge, and startled goats. The staff is used to balance on the bridge and to turn the herd aside by touch and sound, never by a blow.
- Objective (collect): gather a lost caravan's pepper sacks scattered on the harbour road before the evening tide, carrying them in relays.
- Dialogue: Poonkodi: "{name}, a staff is only a longer arm. It keeps trouble away from you, and from those walking behind you." (104 characters)

## Era 4 (proposed). Gupta age, forge road to the iron pillar: the sword
- Setting labels: Gupta empire, c. 320-550 CE (History). The wrought-iron pillar at Delhi, c. 4th to 5th century CE (History, date and origin site debated). A smith's yard on a forge road (Invented, Legend). Sword (khadga) listed among arms in the Dhanurveda tradition (Tradition, date and text version unclear, flagged).
- Earns: the sword: draw, block, guard, and sheathe. A wooden practice blade first, then a plain iron blade the smith forges from {name}'s helping hand at the bellows. Learned from Ayasa (Invented, Legend), a smith who refuses to teach a cut before a block. Inspiration: kalari's wooden-then-metal weapon order (Tradition, documented since about the 12th century CE, used as inspiration only).
- Hurdle: a night of sparks and a toppled cart of hot iron in the forge yard. The blade is used only to block a falling pole and cut a rope that pins a stranded cart. The player is scored on how little is broken.
- Objective (endure): hold a gate bar and keep the forge bellows going until dawn so that a village's bridge nails can be finished, tapping blade on anvil to keep time and stay awake.
- Dialogue: Ayasa: "{name}, a sword that stays sheathed has done its best work. Draw it to shield someone, then put it away." (104 characters)

## Era 5 (proposed). Chola age, Kaveri delta tank and sluice: archery
- Setting labels: Chola empire, c. 850-1279 CE (History). Large temple-and-tank irrigation works of the delta (History, dates of individual tanks not checked). The archery foreshadow: Gupta "archer type" gold coins issued by several Gupta kings in the 4th to 6th centuries CE (History, as reported; the coin in the game is an original drawing, not a copy). The delta village and its watch-tower are Invented, Legend. The Dhanurveda (the "science of the bow") is Tradition, date uncertain.
- Earns: the bow: steady breath, a held draw, aim at what is far, and a call before a release. Learned from Tamarai (Invented, Legend), a tank-watch archer who keeps the sluice flags. Inspiration: Dhanurveda and the temple-tank guard tradition (Tradition, flagged). Arrows are used on rope, flag and target only, never on a person or an animal.
- Hurdle: a flood-night at the tank. A fouled sluice rope is out of reach, and the traveller must send a line across with an arrow to open the gate and ease the water.
- Objective (deliver): shoot a signal line across the swollen channel to a stranded boat, drawing it ashore with its passengers. This is an echo of Mauryan sluice and flood, now solved from a distance.
- Dialogue: Tamarai: "{name}, breathe out, hold, then let go. The arrow only goes as far as the care you gave it." (91 characters)

## How the arc ends (Invented, Legend)
At the last era {name} holds hands, feet, staff, blade and bow, and carries none of them in anger. The hub (prototypes/time-chakra/) can show five small marks for the skills, none of them a trophy. The closing hub line to be written later: "No glory in this. Just a road that stayed open." (docs/story.md).

## Sources
- Silambam, Wikipedia summary (read 2026-11-22): says references in the Silappadikaram and other Sangam literature show the art "since at least the 4th century BC" and that bamboo staffs, swords and armour were sought by foreign traders. It gives no exact dates. Not a primary source.
- Kalaripayattu, Wikipedia summary (read 2026-11-22): Sangam period 600 BCE-300 CE precursors, practised since at least the 12th century CE, training order stances, wooden weapons, metal weapons, bare hands.
- Gupta Coins, Banglapedia (read 2026-11-22): archer-type coins listed for Samudragupta, Chandragupta II, Kumaragupta I, Skandagupta and later kings. It gave no CE dates for them.
- docs/story.md and docs/decisions.md for existing eras and rules.
- Not read or checked: Brahmagupta's text, the Bakhshali manuscript, the Delhi iron pillar, Muziris, the Chola irrigation works, the Dhanurveda text, and any primary source for the Sangam poems.

## Needs scholarly review
- The meaning of shunya: the numeral history (Brahmagupta 628 CE; the placeholder dot in the Bakhshali manuscript, whose radiocarbon dating gave several centuries and has been debated) was written from general knowledge, not checked in a source here. The wording that avoids shunyata's religious sense should be confirmed by a reviewer from those traditions.
- Silambam's age: "since at least the 4th century BCE" is from a Wikipedia summary and may be a loose reading of Sangam texts. The staff trade date is not given.
- Kalaripayattu: origin story credits a deity and a sage, so it is not used on screen. Documented practice only from about the 12th century CE, so the game uses it only as a training-order idea, never as a source for Sangam or Gupta eras.
- Muziris and the Roman pepper trade: dates and the site's identity were not checked. The Sangam date range (300 BCE-300 CE) varies by scholar.
- The Delhi iron pillar: date and original location are debated and were not checked.
- Gupta archer coins: the specific kings and CE dates were not given by the source read. The depiction of a ruler on a coin must be an original drawing and no real king may appear as a character (docs/decisions.md, item 1).
- The Dhanurveda: the date and which weapons it lists vary by text version. The sword and archery lines in the game are Tradition and flagged.
- Chola irrigation: which tank and which century were not checked. A reviewer should pick a real tank or keep it unnamed.
- Respect check: staff, sword and bow teaching as a path "from nothing" should be read by someone from the practising communities, to confirm it does not trivialise living martial or devotional traditions.
- All invented teachers' names (Suvrata is existing; Revati, Poonkodi, Ayasa, Tamarai are new) should be checked for fit with the region and period.
